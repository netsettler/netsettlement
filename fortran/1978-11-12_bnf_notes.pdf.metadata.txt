I really cringe at looking at this particular document. This is from
1978 when I was just starting work on the translator. It may or may
not be correct or complete as to what the translator did but at the
time I was just learning about languages and bnfs and compilers, so it
seemed the right way to express the output. In years following, I
would come to eschew thinking about anything Lispy in terms of this
kind of bnf, and instead would have described the output in structural
form (symbols, numbers, cons cells, etc., which each have their own
print structure, but that isn't always best described in this kind of
bnf form, since the lisp reader is more dynamic in nature). But this
is best seen as a step in my evolution of understanding and typical of
how people talked about languages in the day.
 --Kent Pitman 15-Nov-2026 (cringing)
