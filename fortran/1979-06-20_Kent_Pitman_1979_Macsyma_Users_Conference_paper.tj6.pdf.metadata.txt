I believe this document is in TJ6 format, an early text justifier
markup that was capable of displaying to the Xerox Graphics Printer
(XGP) available at the MIT AI Lab.

This is the source to my paper that appears in the proceedings of the
1979 Macsyma Users Conference. I can't say for sure that it is the
precisely identical version, but I'm guessing probably it is.

This text is on fanfold paper of the sort that would have been
obtained from an LA36 paper console / printer. Those pages did not fit
in my scanner. In most cases, that meant I had to scan the top part of
the page separate from the bottom, so the pages in this PDF
considerably overlap.

 - PDF page 1 is the top 2/3 of paper page 1,
   PDF page 2 is the bottom 2/3 of paper page 1,
   PDF page 3 is the top 2/3 of page 2, and
   PDF page 4 is the bottom 2/3 of page 2.

 - There is one page where the line was so long that
   I needed a third page to catch the overflow.
   (That page will be obvious because it does not
   start at the left edge.)

 - The final PDF page is the top 2/3 of the final
   paper page. There was not a full page of text,
   so no bottom image needed in the PDF.

The characters (like ^F) introduced by "^" are control characters. For
example:

 - Control-F followed by a digit selected the corresponding font.
   For example, ^F0 selects font 0. See the .FONT 0 declaration at the
   start of the file.

 - Control-Q quoted the character following. For example, this was needed
   for ^Q^Y to tell TJ6 to send an ASCII control-Y (decimal code 25), which
   in the SAIL (Stanford AI Lab) character set meant right-pointing arrow.

TJ6 coupled with the XGP were able to produce what was called
"camera-ready copy" that could go straight into the proceedings
without re-typesetting.
