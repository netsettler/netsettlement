# Conferences

## What Is Here and What Is Not

I had a lot of proceedings and did not scan most of that. In some
cases I just had to trust that real publishers (like the ACM) were
responsible for preserving their part of history.

For the more vulnerable conferences, the home grown ones, I tried
to pay more attention.

It varies a lot by venue what got saved. The kinds of artifacts you'll
find here:

* Some informally published proceedings

* Some marketing material, including calls for papers

* Some brochures that were handed out to people attending

* Whatever other scraps I happened to save that seemed relevant.

## My Notes

In some cases there are notes by me. I shouldn't have to say these things,
but I will do so merely for emphasis:

* Except as expressly called out otherwise, I'm including complete documents
  in the sense of "what I have available". (I can't vouch for whether I lost
  pages, and there could be places in which I have the sense that it's better
  for some reason not to include a full document.)

* Where there are phone numbers or email addresses, I sometimes try to
  obstruct them if I think they are still likely to be active and something
  that could intrude personally. If it's an organization, I probably don't
  bother. But you shouldn't call or email people just because you see their
  contact info here. That's hopefully common sense.

* My notes are at best my opinions, never offered as absolute facts.

* Often it's me listening to a talk, writing down something that was
  said. Maybe something I thought was said that was me mishearing
  something. Do not assume I wrote things down correctly.

* Sometimes something said in a talk spurred me to think of
  something unrelated in that moment that I wanted to write down,
  something that was not in the talk.

* Sometimes for my conference notes (and some of them were very long),
  there were three different forms:

  * The original hand-written notes.
    
  * The transcribed notes in full (usually long) form, often more
    than a dozen pages.
    
  * A short form I called an executive summary or some such that
    was a 'highlights' version that was maybe only 1-3 pages, or so.

* Regarding audience of the notes:

  * Often, not always, I was the intended audience of my notes.
    In such cases, I was writing for my own purposes

  * Sometimes I was writing a document to be used as a trip report for
    others in my organization who did not attend. It was expensive to
    send people, and sometimes a requirement to write a trip report,
    but I think also I felt I owed the people who didn't get the
    privilege of being there some sense of what they missed.

  * At no time was I writing for the purposes of you reading this in a
    decades-later historical archive. :) You're just getting a window
    into an earlier time period in the raw form it happened.
