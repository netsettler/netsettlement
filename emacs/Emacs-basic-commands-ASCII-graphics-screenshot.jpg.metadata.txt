An ASCII graphics drawing of how EMACS commands are arranged
directionally, like a compass, with "Cursor" at the middle and lines
outward at the north, east, south, and west directions. There are two
levels of distance forming rings around that. "Previous Line
(Control-P)" and "Next Line" (Control-N) are to the near north and
south, respectively. "Move Backward (Control-B)" and "Move Forward
(Control-F)" are to the near west and east, respectively. Then
"Previous Screen (Meta-V)" and "Next Screen (Control-V)" are to the
far north and south, respectively. And "Head of Line (Control-A)" and
"End of Line (Control-E)" are to the far west and east, respectively."
In the blank space at the corners are additional notations noting
Rubout or Delete deletes backward, Control-D deletes forward,
Control-T twiddles characters, and Control-K kills to end of
line. There is a note at the very top left saying that Meta-Less-Than
goes to the beginning of the buffer and another at the bottom right
saying that Meta-Greater-Than goes to the end of buffer.
