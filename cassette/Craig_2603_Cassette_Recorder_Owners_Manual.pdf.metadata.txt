# Craig 2603 Cassette Recorder

I got mine in probably late 1969 or early 1970, as that was when I was
in 6th grade. My dad was in the military and we got transferred almost
every year growing up, though we were in that location two years. My
next door neighbor Mike (who had one, too) was only there the first
year of that stay, when I was in 6th grade, not when I was in
7th. Changes in my cast of characters, which were frequent, have the
side-effect of locating events in my memories with a fair degree of
temporal accuracy, even as other aspects of the memories are more
fragile.

Mike and I wrote and recorded things that were like radio
plays. Parodies of TV shows, for example. Trying to mimic what Carol
Burnette did with the parodies on her show.

When Mike moved away the next year, in addition to writing long
letters, we also sent cassette tapes in the mail, still making up
things to amuse each other but also just recording voice as people do
on social media or something today, just slower and more expensive
to transmit back then.

And we taped TV shows. That turned out to be useful when I later moved
to the Panama Canal Zone because most TV there was in Spanish and the
only American station had limited content. So it was nice I had a bunch
of Star Trek episodes to listen to over and over.

Anyway, it was a beloved device and well-engineered. The little T
switch got more wobbly with time, but we used it a LOT and that
seemed fair.

 --Kent Pitman, 19 Oct 2026
