# 6.001 Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs

MIT numbers seemingly everything. Buildings, departments, courses.
Course 6 is the Electrical Engineering and Computer Science department.
6.001 (Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs) was taught
by Gerry Sussman and Hal Abelson for which the popular book
_Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs_ was eventually written.

But the class started as 6.031, which taught Algol, Lisp, and an
idealized Machine Language called M. The class ran on a PDP-11/45
that was running the Delphi operating system. They decided to revamp the
course, and there was a bit of an evolution of trying various things
before the course stabilized in the way everyone knows about now.

These are just some random artifacts I had left around. I can't recall
why Algol was still in use for the new course. But there was a transition
time during which a TOPS-20 operating system was used.
