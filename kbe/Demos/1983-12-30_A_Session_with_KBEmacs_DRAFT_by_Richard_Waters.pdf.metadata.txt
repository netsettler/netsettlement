This is a draft. Note several important things:

 * The memo number is AIM-753 (MIT AI Lab Memo 753).

 * The document itself has no date, but the printer says the
   date it was printed was 1983-12-30.

But the historical record is confusing for making sure this information is logged properly.

There IS a later paper called "The Programmer's Apprentice: A Session with KBEmacs" by Waters,
which is much-cited. See citations at:

https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Programmer's-Apprentice%3A-A-Session-with-KBEmacs-Waters/af582b71b26f2ea64ade12bfd7267993577ab448#citing-papers

Alas, it's behind a paywall at IEEE so I cannot verify the
content. But the publication date is November 1, 1985, almost two
years later. Also, I _seriously_ doubt that IEEE published the paper
in this form, with what amount to screenshots. (These are not actual
screenshots, but they are unexplained representations of what you'd
see on the screen, labeled only minimally by information about what
commands would be executed.)  I also doubt, though it's possible, that
the final paper was this detailed.

I could not find an AI Memo 753 in MIT's DSpace.  There IS a paper in
DSpace which calls itself MIT TR-753 (note similar number) and that
paper is about the Programmer's Apprentice, but it is different:
"KBEmacs: A Step Toward the Programmer's Apprentice"
("A.I. T.R. No. 753", May 1985) by Richard C. Waters.

So it's possible this changed a lot in those years. Or it's possible
that something is misfiled. To make sure nothing was lost to history,
I decided to go ahead and add this paper to the public collection.  I
think it represents a materially different view than is likely to be
available in the other documents, and it's likely especially of
interest to historians wanting a window into how these programs could
really be used.
